## Row A — `walk1..8`

**Intent.** She owns the ground she is standing on: a blind zoner gliding in at half speed, sweeping the floor ahead of her with the bo tip held out low like a cane — the player should feel they can stop, poke at max range, or break into the sprint on the very next frame.

**Beats**

1. BEAT 1 - CONTACT (near leg leads). Widest split of the cycle: the camera-side boot lands flat and forward, the far boot still flat behind with the heel just lifting. Spine upright and stacked over the hips, hood level. Head and hood turned three-quarters toward the viewer so both white eyes read; the face is aimed nowhere, never down at her feet. Both gloved hands stacked LOW at hip height on the butt third of the shaft, near hand forward of the far hand. The bo is held out FORWARD and NEAR-HORIZONTAL, about 15 degrees from horizontal, the tip at ankle height ahead of the lead boot and always higher than the soles. Both scarf tails streamed back, hem at its widest flare.
2. BEAT 2 - DOWN (weight passes on). Body at its LOWEST point of the row, about 4 cell-px below beat 1's hood height: lead knee folds and takes the load, far leg straightens behind with the toe dragging its last contact. Torso stays vertical, no forward pitch. Both hands ride the staff DOWN with the body; the tip dips to its lowest gap above the floor without touching it and without dropping below the soles. Hem compresses, scarf tails sag half a beat behind the body.
3. BEAT 3 - PASS (far leg swings through, near leg standing). Feet nearly together under the hips, the far knee lifted and passing BEHIND the near shin, near leg straight, body rising. The far hand pushes the shaft forward a palm-width and the near hand slides back along it — arms visibly re-set, never a static grip. Hood tips slightly BACK, listening up the corridor ahead. Scarf tails drop closest to the body here.
4. BEAT 4 - UP (highest point, reach out). Body at its HIGHEST, about 4 cell-px above beat 1: near leg fully extended on the ball of the foot, far leg reaching forward with the heel leading. Torso still upright. The staff pushes forward and drops a few degrees, the tip stabbing ahead of the reaching heel — she is putting the tip where her foot is about to go. Hem kicks forward, scarf tails lift and bloom.
5. BEAT 5 - CONTACT (far leg leads). NOT a flipped copy of beat 1: the far boot lands flat forward and is drawn crossing IN FRONT of the near leg, which now trails behind — the limb reading as nearest the camera swaps sides, so the silhouette differs from beat 1 at a glance. Hands swap too: the far hand takes the forward low grip, the near hand slides back to the butt. Hood and eye-line at exactly the beat-1 height, head turn held.
6. BEAT 6 - DOWN (off-side load). Lowest point again: front knee folded, rear leg straight with the toe dragging, torso vertical, the tip at its lowest gap above the floor. The trailing near leg is drawn a shade flatter and darker than the lead leg so the two never merge into one shape at this crossing.
7. BEAT 7 - PASS (near leg swings through). Feet together under the hips, but this time the NEAR leg is the one swinging, its knee lifted IN FRONT of the far standing shin — the near/far limb order is the opposite of beat 3, so the two pass beats pack as different drawings. Body rising, staff levelling back toward 15 degrees, hands at beat-3 spacing but on opposite ends of the shaft. Hood tips slightly FORWARD, feeling the air the other way. Scarf tails at their tightest.
8. BEAT 8 - UP (loop seam). Highest point: standing leg extended on the ball of the foot, other leg reaching forward heel-first, the staff pushed forward with the tip ahead of the reaching heel. The reaching foot must be placed so that dropping it flat produces EXACTLY beat 1 — beat 8 flows straight back into beat 1 with no jump in hood height, staff angle, hand placement or head turn.

**Packing:** every beat is a stance, so the default beat-1 anchor is correct. `--dry` first.

## Row B — `airhurt1..8` — the launched hurt arc

**Intent.** The player should feel her body stop being hers: a small robed rag torn off the floor, folded over in the air and dumped back down - with the bo swinging off her fists like something she is no longer holding on purpose.

**Beats**

1. BEAT 1 - THE POP (rising hardest). Both boots torn off the floor and still low, toes pointing down and trailing. Spine snapped into a hard back-arch, the hood flung back flat against the skull but never off, chin up. Both white eyes still flat, open and aimed nowhere - no squeeze, no roll, no reaction; she never saw it coming and cannot look at it now. Both gloved hands still ON the bo but as loose hooked fists at the balance point; the staff has already dropped out of guard and hangs diagonally below her hips, butt down.
2. BEAT 2 - RISING (still hard up). The reversed-C deepens - hips leading, shoulders behind them. Both arms trail straight DOWN below the torso, elbows soft and inverted, the bo hanging almost vertical beneath her from the loose fists. Robe hem snapped straight down, both scarf tails whipped dead-vertical under the chin. No muscle tone anywhere: knees passively folded at slightly different angles, feet dangling, ankles loose.
3. BEAT 3 - RISE EASING. The arch relaxes out of the spine into limpness; the head lolls back and to the side so the hood opening points off the line of travel, sagging open. Arms begin to float up past the hips as the rise slows; the bo swings from vertical toward a steep diagonal, still hanging from the same two loose fists. Hem and scarf start to lose their downward stretch.
4. BEAT 4 - APEX, TURNING OVER. Weightless and boneless: the whole body drifting toward face-up horizontal, shoulders BELOW the hips, the neck slack so the head trails behind the shoulders instead of leading them. Knees half-open at two clearly different angles, both ankles dropped, toes NOT pointed, fingers hooked open rather than gripping. Arms float up level with the torso at two different heights, the bo drifting across her waist to a shallow diagonal at an angle nothing in her body supports. Robe hem and both scarf tails bloom outward in all directions, one tail caught across the hood. This is the one beat with NO line of tension in it - not a single straight limb, not one braced joint.
5. BEAT 5 - APEX, PAST THE TOP. Rolled a few degrees further, head now the lowest part of the torso and hanging off the neck like a weight, one knee floating clearly higher than the other, one fist slid a palm-width along the shaft so the bo hangs unevenly across her. Nothing symmetrical, nothing braced, no pose: this beat must look like something DROPPED, not something held. Explicitly forbidden here - a level head, a straight spine, matched knees, pointed toes, a closed fist, a hand reaching toward the floor, or any read of a floating ninja.
6. BEAT 6 - THE FOLD (falling begins). The torso jack-knifes forward over the hips: head drops below hip level, the back rounds, both legs kick up behind at uneven angles. Arms swing up past the head and the bo is dragged up above her, trailing from the loose fists like an anchor on a rope. Hem peels upward off the legs, scarf tails reverse and stream up.
7. BEAT 7 - FALLING. Fully folded and head-low, hips high, knees loose and streaming upward, arms fully overhead. The bo trails straight up above her at a steep diagonal, still in both loose fists. Hood pressed flat against the back of the skull, robe pulled taut upward along the body - every drawn line points up while she goes down.
8. BEAT 8 - FALLING HARD (handoff to the floor). Near-vertical head-down drop: the body has straightened out of the fold into one long falling line, head lowest, feet highest, arms and the bo all streaming straight up past her. Maximum downward read - the silhouette is a dropped plumb line, not a fighter. Still airborne. Nothing in this beat may look recoverable or composed.
