## Row A — `walk1..8`

**Intent.** The sentence is already written — he is only walking over to read it to you. A heavy mid-speed tank covering real ground without ever hurrying, one thumb from the draw at every step, the helmet and the two horns held dead level while everything below the belt does the work.

**Beats**

1. BEAT 1 — LEFT CONTACT. ## Row B — `airhurt1..8` — the launched hurt arc

**Intent.** For eight frames the most composed body in the game stops being a person and becomes a dropped coat. No pose anywhere in the row — every frame is a thing that has lost its argument with physics, and the only expression permitted is the amber light going half-out near the bottom of the arc.

**Beats**

1. BEAT 1 — THE POP. Both boots torn off the floor and still trailing straight down below him, toes pointed at the ground, knees locked; spine arched hard BACKWARD into a C; helmet snapped back so the horns point down-and-behind and the black hood opening is thrown up at the sky; both arms flung up and back past the shoulder line, hands open and empty; the sheathed sword rides thrust through the obi at his waist and has kicked out horizontally from the hip; scarf tails snapped straight DOWN because he is rising faster than the cloth.
2. BEAT 2 — RISING HARD. The backward arch deepens until the shoulders are below the hip line and the body has begun a slow backward rotation; legs trailing now, knees soft and splayed at different angles; the arms have gone past full extension and the shoulders read pulled out of their sockets; the sheathed sword swings on the obi knot with the copper cap flung out and up; scarf tails streaming straight down and whipping at their ends.
3. BEAT 3 — RISE SLOWING. The arch unloads — the spine goes from a C to nearly straight and for one frame he is a falling plank; the arms are still above the shoulder line but folding at the elbow, wrists broken and hands flopping; the head lolls forward off the back-snap, chin dropping; the legs drift apart with one knee riding noticeably higher than the other; the scarf loses its straight line and begins to curl.
4. BEAT 4 — APEX, WEIGHTLESS. The rag frame. No muscle tone anywhere and no line of intent through the body: the shoulders and the hips are on two different axes, so no single spine can be traced from head to boots. He hangs almost horizontal and face-up, limbs splayed and hanging BELOW the torso from dead shoulders and dead hips; both hands open with the fingers apart and slightly curled the wrong way; knees and ankles loose, one boot turned in and one turned out; the helmet has slipped off the line of the neck at an angle no neck makes, horns aimed at the floor; the sheathed sword hangs almost straight down out of the obi with the copper cap the lowest point of the whole silhouette; the scarf floats in a slack catenary going neither up nor down. If you can name this pose, redraw it.
5. BEAT 5 — THE TIP-OVER. The hips fall first and the torso starts rotating from face-up toward face-down; the arms have not caught up and now trail ABOVE the body, still limp; the head is the last thing to come round and is still lolling back; the sheath swings up past the ribs as the hips drop out from under it.
6. BEAT 6 — FALLING. The body folds forward at the waist, chest coming toward the knees; both arms fully trailing straight UP above the shoulders, hands open; the head is down between the arms with the horns aimed at the floor; the legs have swung up behind him; scarf tails stream straight UP; the sheath has flipped so the copper cap points up.
7. BEAT 7 — INVERTED. He has rotated PAST vertical: the helmet is now below the hips, the whole silhouette upside-down and compressed into a tight comma with every limb dragged up and behind him. One boot sole is the highest point of the shape. The black hood opening is aimed at the ground and the amber eyes are half-lidded and dimmed — the only expression permitted in the row. Scarf a straight vertical line above him.
8. BEAT 8 — TERMINAL. The comma blows back open under air drag: spine stretched, legs straight out behind, both arms straight above, the whole body at full vertical stretch head-down toward the floor — the tallest, thinnest silhouette in the row. The sheath trails straight up out of the obi knot. Scarf a hard straight vertical line above him. This is the fastest-falling frame and must read as the bottom of a monotone arc that began at beat 1.
