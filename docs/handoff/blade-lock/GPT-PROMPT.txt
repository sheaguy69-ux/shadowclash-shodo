================================ PART 1 — every set ================================

You are drawing new animation cells for a 2D fighting game called Shadow Clash.

THE MEDIUM — read first
Clean high-contrast 2D CEL ILLUSTRATION / vector look. Heavy black outlines, readable
silhouette, controlled dark palette, glowing featureless angled eyes. THIS IS NOT PIXEL
ART — do not pixelate, do not posterize, do not reduce the palette, do not add a pixel
grid. Chibi proportions (big head, short limbs), matching the reference exactly.
Flat WHITE background. PNG. 2K minimum on the long edge.

⛔ Every cell is a PURE SIDE PROFILE facing LEFT — the character looks toward the LEFT
edge of the image. Not right. LEFT. Match the direction the reference image faces exactly.
Never draw a right-facing or three-quarter view.

WHAT I NEED — TEN cells of a BLADE LOCK
Two fighters' weapons have caught against each other and they are straining, like the
deadlock struggle in a samurai duel.

You are drawing ONE fighter, ALONE in the frame. Do NOT draw two fighters. The opponent
is NOT in the image — this fighter is pressing against someone invisible, off to the
LEFT (the direction they are facing). The game slides two separate drawings together until their weapons meet.

  1    CATCH. The weapons have just met. Weight arriving forward over the front foot, both
       arms braced, head up. The frame of impact, not the swing before it.
  2    SETTLE. The catch absorbs. Elbows compress, rear foot digs in, shoulders drop.
  3-8  STRAIN — six frames of ONE continuous held push. These LOOP on screen, so they must
       read as ONE POSE BREATHING: the body creeping deeper, the weapon trembling, weight
       shifting between the feet. Not six different poses, and not six identical drawings.
  9    WIN. Breaks the bind open. Front foot steps THROUGH, weapon drives forward and up
       past the contact point, chest opens, full extension.
  10   LOSE. Thrown off. Torso rocks back, weapon knocked wide off the centre line, guard
       broken open, back foot catching the weight. Off balance but STILL ON BOTH FEET —
       this is not a knockdown.

  (Ten cells rather than six because the strain LOOPS: at the engine's 9fps loop rate a
  bind lasting 1.15s cycles a 3-frame loop 3.4 times, which reads as a stutter. Six strain
  frames cycle 1.7 times and read as one continuous strain. The engine takes any count.)

⛔ THIS IS A STRAINING BIND, NOT A THRUST — the correction that mattered most on the
first batch. Every one of the first five sets came back as a straight-armed poke: weapon
held out at full arm's length with an upright body behind it. That is a thrust, and it is
wrong. The fighter is pressing HARD against an invisible opponent's weapon that is pushing
BACK:
  - weight pitched forward ONTO the front foot, not sitting back over the hips
  - elbows BENT and compressed, weapon held CLOSE to the body, not extended
  - rear leg straight and braced, rear foot dug into the ground
  - shoulders low and driving forward, head up
  - in cells 3-8 the body is ONE STRAIGHT LINE from the rear heel to the weapon
Think tug-of-war, or a rugby scrum — a body committed against resistance. Cells 9 and 10
are the only ones where the arms extend.

⛔ SIZE — THE ONLY SIZE RULE THAT MATTERS

Every cell in this set must have the figure at EXACTLY the same body height. Measure from
the soles of the feet to the crown of the head — INCLUDING hair, hood and horns, EXCLUDING
the weapon. That measurement must be identical in all cells, to within 1%.

The POSE changes; the SIZE does not. If a pose does not fit, make the CANVAS bigger — never
scale the figure to fit.

⛔ Do NOT try to match this fighter's size to any OTHER fighter's. That is corrected
automatically after delivery, by rescaling the whole set against the fighter's existing
in-game art. Only INTERNAL consistency matters here — and it is the one thing that cannot
be corrected afterwards, because a single uniform scale cannot reconcile cells that were
drawn at different sizes. A set whose cells disagree by more than 6% is rejected outright.

⛔ THE CONTACT POINT — MID-CHEST, AND LEVEL. THIS IS THE MOST COMMON FAILURE.
(Why it is strict here specifically: these cells are HELD on screen for over a second with
both fighters visible and nearly still, and each fighter is drawn ALONE then slid into place
by the game. The weapons only touch if every fighter puts their weapon on the SAME line. A
fast clash animation would not care; a held bind cares completely.)
In cells 1-8 the weapon's binding edge must sit at ONE fixed spot:

  HEIGHT   just UNDER HALF the figure's total body height, measured up from the feet.
           On a figure drawn 1000px foot-to-head that is about 480px up — the MIDDLE OF
           THE CHEST. Level, roughly horizontal.
  FORWARD  ahead of the body toward the LEFT edge, the side they face.

⛔ NOT UP BESIDE THE HEAD. The first kael attempt crossed his blades at roughly 80% of his
body height with the tips angled upward. Every fighter binds on the SAME world line, and a
short fighter holding a long staff still holds it at mid-chest — so a bind drawn at head
height cannot meet the opponent at all. It simply misses.

⛔ IT MUST NOT DRIFT between cells 1-8 — same height, same distance forward, in all eight.
The body leans and compresses AROUND it; the contact point itself does not move.

⛔ NO EFFECTS. AT ALL. Violated in two batches out of four, so stated exhaustively:
no speed lines, no impact flashes, no GLOW, no sparks, no debris or floating chips, no
strain ticks or vibration marks, no dust, no sweat drops.

The game draws all of its own effects and composites these cells directly, so anything drawn
in sits FROZEN on screen for over a second as an obvious artifact.

Detached specks are deleted automatically at pack time — but GLOW FUSED TO A WEAPON cannot be
separated from it and is PERMANENT. Draw the pose and nothing else.

METHOD — DO THIS, IT MATTERS MOST
Do NOT generate ten independent images. Work in a CHAIN, each one edited from the last:

   reference -> 1 -> 2 -> 3 -> 4 -> 5 -> 6 -> 7 -> 8
                         3 -> 9     (win branches off the strain)
                         3 -> 10    (lose branches off the strain)

Each step is a SMALL change to an image that already holds the pose. Independent images
drift in body size and the animation boils on screen. If one cell comes out wrong,
redo THAT ONE from its parent — never the whole set.

Draw the weapon I name and no other. Do not add weapons, do not change the number of
blades, do not add extra limbs. Keep the palette, outline weight, mask, hood, wraps and
every accessory exactly as the reference shows them.

FIGHTER: KAEL — youngest of the roster. Weapon for this set: the LONG KATANA, and he

FIGHTER: EMBER — weapon: IRON CLAWS (tekko-kagi) on BOTH hands, NOT a sword. He does not

back with green energy fused to the claw tips, which cannot be stripped out afterwards.

FIGHTER: MIZU — visibly the shortest fighter, and she holds the LONGEST weapon: one long

FIGHTER: SHIN — weapon for this set: a KUNAI held in his hand (not thrown). Short reach,
